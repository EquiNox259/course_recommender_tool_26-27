Course Recommender — Documentation

A technical reference for the DAV Team Course Recommender, IIT Bombay

1 Introduction

The Course Recommender helps IITB undergraduates discover elective courses that align with both their academic history and their stated research interests. It combines two complementary signals to generate personalised recommendations:

  • People's Score (PS) — captures collective intelligence by analysing enrollment overlap across the student body. It answers: "What courses did students with a similar history tend to take?"
  • Interest Similarity Score (ISS) — uses semantic embeddings to match course content against the student's stated research interests and skills. It answers: "What courses are most relevant to what I actually want to learn?"

The final recommendation score is a weighted sum of PS and ISS, with the weights controlled by a slider so each student can tune the balance to their preference. After scoring, every candidate course is passed through a multi-layer eligibility engine before being shown.

ⓘ  The recommender operates on the currently running semester's course list. Eligibility checks reflect the student's academic history, department restrictions, prerequisites, and time-slot constraints for that semester.

2 Score Computation

2.1 People's Score (PS)

The People's Score measures co-enrollment similarity between two courses, based on seniors' inputs. For a course h in the student's history and a candidate course c:

$$ PS(h,\, c) = \frac{|\,Students(h)\; \cap\; Students(c)\,|}{|\,Students(h)\; \cup\; Students(c)\,|} $$
Students(h) = set of students who took course h  |  Students(c) = set of students who took course c
✓  A high PS indicates that students who followed a similar academic path frequently enrolled in this course, a strong signal of suitability within a given department and degree programme.

2.2 Interest Similarity Score (ISS)

The Interest Similarity Score involves matching course descriptions with the interest(s) of students using cosine similarity.

✓  A high ISS indicates that the course's content is very closely related to the students' interests, regardless of whether their seniors have historically taken the course.

2.3 Final Score & Slider

The two scores are combined into a single Final Score (FS) via a weighted sum. The weight parameter α can be decided by the student by means of a slider.

$$ FS \;=\; \alpha \cdot ISS \;+\; (1-\alpha) \cdot PS \qquad \alpha \in [0,\, 1] $$
α = Semantic (ISS) weight  |  1−α = People's Score (PS) weight

The slider positions map to the following intent:

⬅ More Interest-Based More Safer Route ➡
Semantic Weight: 50% People's Score Weight: 50%
  • Far left (α → 1): prioritise courses that match stated interests. Best for students exploring a new area.
  • Far right (α → 0): recommendations driven entirely by what similar students have taken. Best for students following a well-established path (within their department).
  • Centre (α = 0.5): balanced blend — the default setting.

The top 10 courses by FS are then forwarded to the eligibility engine.

3 Eligibility Engine

Every recommended course passes through a sequential set of checks. Results are grouped into four sections based on the first blocking condition encountered:

✓ Eligible
Passes all checks. Available to register.
ⓘ Instructor Consent Required
Prerequisites met but instructor approval is required or conditional.
⏳ Time Slot Clash
This course clashes with an upcoming core course.
⚠ Not Eligible
Fails restriction or prerequisite. Additional conditions shown.

5 Summary

  • ✓ Recommendations are generated by combining interest matching (ISS) with prior enrollment patterns (PS) into a tunable Final Score.
  • ✓ The α slider lets students shift emphasis from pure interest exploration to safer, well-trodden academic paths.
  • ✓ Every recommended course is validated against department restrictions, prerequisite expressions, and time-slot constraints before display.
  • ✓ Division-level navigation allows students to check time-slot clash, for their specific division.
